# M01: task suite, interfaces, and validation

M01 implements a small Gymnasium cue-matching task with three observation
variants, independent memory and reward delays, separate data partitions, a
causal recurrent-policy interface, and reproducible diagnostic records.
The retained experiment passes the milestone gate. This document describes
the implemented behavior and the evidence supporting that decision.

## Scope and hypothesis

The hypothesis is that remembering a cue is necessary when the current
observation hides it, while delaying a fixed decision's reward changes its
discounted magnitude without changing which decision is best.

M01 has no earlier milestone dependencies. Its diagnostic policies are
hand-written; there is no trained controller, neural encoder, Hopfield update,
optimizer, replay, or feature cache yet. Those components belong to later
milestones. The visual decoder establishes task solvability and is not evidence
that a pretrained visual encoder preserves the cue.

The gate requires at least 95% success from a policy with sufficient information,
a measured random-policy reference, demonstrable history dependence, preserved
decision optimality across payout delays, disjoint partitions, and causal inputs.
Architecture experiments must stop if solvability or the information audit fails.

## Task dynamics

Each episode begins with a uniformly sampled red or blue cue. Two cards remain
visible: one on the left and one on the right. The layout specifies which color
is on the left; the other color is on the right. Choose the side matching the cue.
The cue sample is independent of the chosen layout. There are no distractors,
movement dynamics, penalties, or intermediate rewards.

Let `D = cue_delay` and `R = reward_delay`:

- `D` is the number of environment transitions between observing the cue at
  reset and observing the choice state. It must be an integer from 1 to 64.
- `R` is the number of transitions after the choice transition before payout.
  It must be an integer from 0 to 64. Zero pays on the choice transition itself.
- A naturally completed episode has exactly `D + 1 + R` transitions.
- The default is `D=4`, `R=0`, with no external time limit.

The cue appears at observation index `t=0`. There is one compulsory WAIT action
at each index before `t=D`; the LEFT/RIGHT decision is made at `t=D`.
For `R>0`, the choice is committed and only WAIT is allowed until payout.

| Observation index | Phase | Available action | Information/event |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0` | CUE | WAIT | Cue and both cards visible |
| `1` through `D-1` | DELAY | WAIT | Cue hidden in memory variants |
| `D` | DECISION | LEFT or RIGHT | One irrevocable choice |
| `D+1` through `D+R` | PAYOFF | WAIT | Past choice visible, reward pending |
| `D+1+R` | TERMINAL | None | Reward emitted on the preceding transition |

The DELAY range is empty when `D=1`; the PAYOFF range is empty when `R=0`.
For example, `D=4, R=3` requires four WAIT actions, one choice, and three further
WAIT actions. Payout occurs on the eighth transition. No action can accelerate
the timeline or alter a committed choice.

Compulsory waiting prevents the agent from recording the cue by moving or
choosing an early side. The environment does not store a choice until DECISION.

## Actions and masks

| Value | Action | Legal phases |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | WAIT | CUE, DELAY, PAYOFF |
| 1 | LEFT | DECISION |
| 2 | RIGHT | DECISION |

The action space is `Discrete(3)`. Every observation contains an `int8` mask of
shape `(3,)`, ordered WAIT, LEFT, RIGHT. Its values are `[1,0,0]` for compulsory
waiting, `[0,1,1]` for the decision, and `[0,0,0]` for a true terminal observation.
Masks depend only on the phase, never on the correct side.

Out-of-space or masked actions raise `ValueError` without advancing the state.
Calling `step` before reset or after termination/truncation raises `RuntimeError`.
Exploration and training policies must apply the mask before sampling; do not
sample unmasked actions from `action_space` for a rollout.

The tests use Gymnasium's seeded-reset and passive observation/step checks on
legal trajectories. The general `check_env` routine samples unmasked actions,
so it is not the checker used for this strict masked-action contract.

## Rewards and discount

Success receives `+1` at natural payout; failure receives `0`. All earlier
rewards are zero. Both success and failure end at the same fixed natural horizon.
The primary metric is completed-episode success. Discounted return uses
`gamma=0.99` in the evaluation runner; the environment emits undiscounted rewards.

With zero-based transition rewards, the reset-time return of a correct decision
is `gamma ** (D + R)`. At the choice, its value is `gamma ** R`; an incorrect
choice is worth zero. For every tested finite delay and positive gamma, the
matching card remains the unique best choice. Longer payout delay lowers the
positive return but does not change its maximizer. No reward shaping compensates
for this decrease.

Keep `R` fixed when studying memory by varying `D`; keep `D` fixed when studying
credit assignment by varying `R`. The validation matrix includes `(D,R)` values
`(1,0)`, `(4,0)`, `(4,8)`, and `(16,8)`. The separate exhaustive audit also checks
reward delays 0, 1, 8, and 64.

## Observation variants and exact feature schema

`TaskConfig.variant` selects one of these versions of identical underlying
dynamics and rewards:

| Variant | Observation payload | Cue availability |
|---|---|---|
| `full_state` | `features: float32[16]` | Throughout the episode |
| `delayed_cue` | `features: float32[16]` | At reset only |
| `visual` | `image: uint8[96,96,3]` | At reset only, rendered as an RGB card |

Every variant also supplies `action_mask`. Visual observations have no parallel
symbolic feature vector. Full state exposes the cue, card colors, phase, past
choice, and clocks sufficient to determine transitions and payout; it does not
include an explicit answer label. It is intentionally an information-rich control.

The symbolic vector is fixed and ordered as follows. Every entry lies in `[0,1]`:

| Python slice | Meaning | Encoding |
|---|---|---|
| `0:5` | Phase | One-hot CUE, DELAY, DECISION, PAYOFF, TERMINAL |
| `5:7` | Cue | One-hot red/blue; both zero when hidden |
| `7:9` | Left card | One-hot red/blue |
| `9:11` | Right card | One-hot red/blue |
| `11:14` | Committed choice | One-hot none, left, right |
| `14` | Cue countdown | `max(D-t, 0) / 64` |
| `15` | Payout countdown | `min(R, max(D+1+R-t, 0)) / 64` |

The payout countdown stays at `R/64` until the choice, then counts down on WAIT
transitions. Counts use a fixed scale, so no fitted normalization statistics
are involved. Configured delays and the static task definition are known to the
experiment; episode labels, layout IDs, and episode seeds are not policy inputs.

The visual frame draws only delayed-cue information: a phase marker at rows
2–7, the cue at rows 12–31 when visible, the two cards, a past-choice marker
at rows 70–73, and exact countdown bars at rows 80–83 and 88–91. Cards occupy
left columns 8–31 and right columns 64–87. The RGB palette is red `(230,65,65)`
and blue `(65,125,230)`. Grayscale backgrounds and white markers carry no cue
identity. Rendering never branches on whether a pending choice was correct.

`render()` also produces this delayed-cue view for a full-state environment;
the full-state cue remains available through its symbolic observation.

The encoder's observation window is exactly one current frame or feature vector.
No video stack, future frame, future reward, success label, or simulator-derived
answer is passed to a normal policy. M02 will choose and validate an actual
frozen encoder; M01 implements only the observation source.

## Recurrent policy interface and state lifetimes

`RecurrentPolicy` exposes two methods:

```python
initial_state() -> state
act(inputs: PolicyInput, state) -> (action: int, next_state)
```

`PolicyInput` contains exactly the current observation, `previous_action`,
`previous_reward`, and `episode_start`. At reset these are respectively the reset
observation, `None`, `0.0`, and `True`. Later calls receive only the action and
reward from the already completed previous transition and `episode_start=False`.
`info`, environment objects, episode seeds, layout IDs, and diagnostic labels are
excluded. A policy's explicit state is its sole persistent history channel.

The interface leaves state shape to the controller. In M01 `CueMemoryPolicy`
uses `None` or a red/blue integer. Later controllers can supply a container holding
separate actor state, executive state, history summary, and refresh phase through
the same interface. Their tensor shapes and update schedules must be declared
in their own milestone records; M01 does not instantiate them.

At both termination and truncation, clear policy state, previous action/reward,
and any frame window before the next reset observation. An episode boundary
must not carry a cue into a new episode. Learned weights, frozen encoder weights,
and global prototypes survive resets. Future per-episode episodic banks must
reset separately; replay may persist but must retain episode boundaries and
respect split ownership. A model update has a different lifetime from a state reset.

The runner initializes recurrent state for every episode. `CueMemoryPolicy` also
honors `episode_start`, so callers cannot accidentally reuse a cue when signaling
a new episode. Random-policy RNG streams continue across episodes within a
condition; reseeding that stream every episode is unnecessary.

## True endings, time limits, and value targets

Gymnasium's five-item step result is
`(observation, reward, terminated, truncated, info)`.

Natural payout is a true ending: `terminated=True`, `truncated=False`.
An optional positive `TaskConfig.time_limit` ends collection after that many
transitions before payout: `terminated=False`, `truncated=True`. If the limit
equals or exceeds natural episode length, the true ending takes precedence.
A truncated episode emits no pending reward and has no success diagnostic label.

The observation returned on truncation is the actual continuation observation,
with its legal mask and clocks intact. It is suitable for value estimation;
it is not replaced by a reset observation. A true terminal observation has
TERMINAL phase and no legal actions.

`transition_masks(terminated, truncated)` returns:

- `bootstrap = float(not terminated)`
- `carry_state = float(not (terminated or truncated))`

A one-step value target is `reward + gamma * bootstrap * V(final_observation)`.
For a recurrent value model, evaluate this final observation with the appropriate
continuing history before clearing state. Time limits allow bootstrap while
ending the collected recurrent sequence. Both boundary types stop advantage
recursion across the boundary, even when the truncated final value is bootstrapped.
Never substitute the next episode's reset observation in that target.

M01 calculates observed episodic returns, not value targets. Its validation run
has no time limits. Truncated diagnostic rollouts count as unsuccessful in the
runner, since they did not complete; later reports should also present truncation
counts separately. The tests cover limits before the choice, at the choice,
during payoff, at payout, and beyond payout.

## Layout and seed partitions

Partitions are enforced by `splits.py`. Layouts differ in cue position,
card height, background, and left/right color order. Both orders appear in every
partition so a color-order shortcut does not solve the task.

| Split | Episode seed range, inclusive | Layout | Left color | Cue x | Cards y | Gray background |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pilot | 0–999 | pilot-a | red | 32 | 40 | 16 |
| pilot | 0–999 | pilot-b | blue | 40 | 44 | 20 |
| train | 10,000–99,999 | train-a | red | 24 | 48 | 24 |
| train | 10,000–99,999 | train-b | blue | 48 | 40 | 28 |
| held_out | 1,000,000–1,099,999 | test-a | red | 28 | 44 | 32 |
| held_out | 1,000,000–1,099,999 | test-b | blue | 44 | 48 | 36 |

The cue card is 20 pixels wide. Card dimensions and palette remain fixed;
held-out layouts test simple appearance/position changes. Symbolic observations
omit the visual placement/background, so layout generalization in that variant
reduces to the same underlying card-matching semantics.

Explicit reset seeds outside the selected range and layout overrides from another
partition raise errors. `reset(seed=None)` is permitted for interactive use but
is nondeterministic and must not be used in recorded comparisons. The runner
always records explicit seeds and alternates its split's layouts equally.

With `--episodes 200`, pilot episodes use seeds 0–199 and held-out episodes use
1,000,000–1,000,199. Training layouts and seeds remain reserved. Whole trajectories
belong to one partition; do not randomly split frames into train and test sets.

Policy sampling seeds 101, 202, and 303 are separate RNG streams for the random
policy. They are not environment seeds or independent training seeds. There are
zero training runs in M01. Later learning experiments should use the plan's
pilot/confirmatory training protocol and record a distinct environment-seed schedule.

The same environment conditions are reused across policies and delay variants
for controlled comparisons. Their results are paired and correlated; do not count
all repeated conditions as independent evaluation samples or training runs.

## Diagnostic policies and leakage checks

Four policies are evaluated:

1. **Simulator oracle:** calls `diagnostic_action()` using hidden simulator state.
   This privileged path is isolated to solvability diagnostics.
2. **Cue memory:** reads only current features or pixels, stores the observed cue,
   then matches it against the visible left card at the choice.
3. **Random:** samples uniformly over the current legal mask using its own RNG.
4. **Reactive:** uses the current cue if visible; otherwise always chooses left.
   It retains no cue state.

The visual cue-memory policy uses exact palette matching in fixed regions covering
all layouts. This decoder is intentionally hand-written and untrained. It receives
the same pixels and action mask as a later visual policy.

Reset and nonterminal `info` are empty. Terminal `info` contains only `success`.
The runner records that label after the episode and never passes it to a policy.
Diagnostic reset options `cue=0/1` and `layout_id=...` support paired audits;
they are evaluator controls rather than observation fields.

The memory audit exhausts all six layouts, both memory observation variants,
both cues, and cue delays 1, 4, 16, and 64. In each of 48 paired cases, opposite
cues require opposite actions but produce byte-identical decision observations
and masks. Both episodes also have previous action WAIT, previous reward zero,
and `episode_start=False`. Thus their complete current causal inputs coincide.
Under balanced cues, any policy lacking historical state is bounded by 50%
success. This is a structural check, stronger than testing one reactive policy.

The payout audit enumerates both choices for both cues and all layouts at reward
delays 0, 1, 8, and 64: 48 cue/layout/delay cases, with 96 complete episodes.
It checks exact expected discounted returns and unchanged maximizing actions.
Additional tests compare pre-payout trajectories for correct and incorrect latent
outcomes to ensure no early reward or visual correctness indicator leaks the answer.

## Retained experiment and uncertainty

The retained protocol runs 200 episodes per policy sampling stream and condition,
on pilot and held-out splits, all three variants, and all four delay settings.
Each condition has one oracle, one memory policy, three random streams, and one
reactive policy. There are 144 condition summaries and 28,800 evaluation episodes.
Every episode is retained, including unsuccessful random and reactive episodes.

| Split and variant | Simulator oracle | Cue memory | Random, range over streams | Reactive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot, full state | 100% | 100% | 49.5–52.5% | 100% |
| Pilot, delayed cue | 100% | 100% | 49.5–52.5% | 48.5% |
| Pilot, visual | 100% | 100% | 49.5–52.5% | 48.5% |
| Held out, full state | 100% | 100% | 41–49% | 100% |
| Held out, delayed cue | 100% | 100% | 41–49% | 52% |
| Held out, visual | 100% | 100% | 41–49% | 52% |

Rates were identical across the four delay settings within each listed sampling
stream. The random policy's theoretical probability is 50%; its measured finite
samples are retained as observed, without replacing seeds to improve agreement.
The ranges in this table are descriptive, not confidence intervals.

Each individual condition reports a 95% binomial Wilson interval. For 200/200
successes that interval is approximately `[98.12%, 100%]`. Random and reactive
intervals, discounted returns, standard errors of discounted returns, and
truncation counts are in the JSON record. These intervals describe episode-level
uncertainty for a fixed diagnostic policy, not training-seed uncertainty or an
architecture comparison. No training-seed uncertainty is estimable without training.

Evaluation uses 324,000 environment transitions. The paired memory audit uses
2,040 more, and the reward audit 2,232 more, totaling **328,272 transitions**.
There are 192 audit episodes in addition to the 28,800 evaluation episodes;
the memory-audit episodes intentionally stop at the choice observation.
Retrieval iterations and parameter updates are both zero. The stopping rule is
the fixed budget, with no adaptive retries, tuning trials, or episode exclusions.

The run uses CPU on Windows 11, Python 3.13.5, Gymnasium 1.4.0, and NumPy 2.5.3.
The JSON record includes the processor description and measured wall time.
The wall time covers audits, policies, rendering, and trajectory writing together;
separate encoder latency, neural policy latency, and peak training memory are
not measured because no neural training or encoder is involved.

The result supports continuing to M02/M03: every sufficiently informed policy
passes the 95% gate, both causal memory and payout audits pass, and a reactive
policy behaves as expected when the cue is hidden. It does not establish that
a trainable recurrent policy or a Hopfield actor will solve the task.

## Experiment files and integrity

The retained files are:

- `experiments/m01/record.json`: configuration, hypothesis, gate, reset rules,
  budgets, seeds, stopping rule, audit diagnostics, per-condition results and
  uncertainty, runtime versions, source revision, source hashes, and trajectory hash.
- `experiments/m01/episodes.jsonl.gz`: a losslessly compressed JSON object per
  episode with configuration, policy and sampling seed, environment seed, layout,
  action, full reward sequence, returns, step count, boundary flags, and success.
- `docs/experiment_record_template.md`: reusable protocol/record checklist for
  subsequent milestones, including fields that become relevant when training begins.

Read and verify the retained trajectories using the standard library:

```python
import gzip
import hashlib
import json
from pathlib import Path

directory = Path("experiments/m01")
record = json.loads((directory / "record.json").read_text(encoding="utf-8"))
trajectory_path = directory / record["episodes_file"]
assert hashlib.sha256(trajectory_path.read_bytes()).hexdigest() == record["episodes_sha256"]
with gzip.open(trajectory_path, "rt", encoding="utf-8") as stream:
    episodes = [json.loads(line) for line in stream]
assert len(episodes) == record["episodes"]
```

Gzip header timestamps, record creation times, and wall time can vary on reproduction.
Compare decompressed trajectories and numerical results rather than expecting
the compressed artifact checksum to match a new run. The recorded checksum
verifies the integrity of its own artifact. Source hashes identify the exact
Python files used; the Git revision identifies their committed implementation.

## Using the environment directly

```python
from hopfield_rl.env import CueTaskEnv, TaskConfig
from hopfield_rl.policies import CueMemoryPolicy, PolicyInput

env = CueTaskEnv(TaskConfig(
    variant="visual", split="train", cue_delay=16, reward_delay=8,
))
policy = CueMemoryPolicy()
observation, info = env.reset(seed=10_000)
state = policy.initial_state()
inputs = PolicyInput(observation, episode_start=True)

while True:
    action, state = policy.act(inputs, state)
    observation, reward, terminated, truncated, info = env.step(action)
    if terminated or truncated:
        break
    inputs = PolicyInput(observation, previous_action=action, previous_reward=reward)
env.close()
```

The package uses a `src` layout. Implementation entry points are:

- `src/hopfield_rl/env.py`: configuration, dynamics, feature schema, masks, renderer.
- `src/hopfield_rl/splits.py`: disjoint layouts and seed ranges.
- `src/hopfield_rl/policies.py`: causal interface, reference policies, boundary masks.
- `src/hopfield_rl/evaluate.py`: audits, rollouts, statistics, records, and CLI.
- `tests/test_env.py` and `tests/test_evaluate.py`: 50 passing checks covering
  dynamics, timing boundaries, hidden-cue aliasing, visual causality, split
  isolation, policy reset, vector observations, and record integrity.

## Limits and next milestones

This task has two cue identities, one choice per episode, deterministic transitions,
a shared palette, and small finite delays. It is an intentionally controlled
diagnostic task. It does not establish navigation, distractor robustness, motion
understanding, long-horizon training, or broad visual generalization. Its action
masks reveal the decision schedule but not the correct answer.

The delay cap of 64 makes countdown rendering exact and keeps the first suite
small. Increasing that cap later requires an explicit schema/rendering change
and a new record. Layout appearance is fixed within each episode. None of the
M01 policies learn from training data; examining held-out solvability here does
not justify tuning later learned models on these held-out episodes.

M02 should check that frozen visual features retain the cue using separate
training and held-out trajectories. M03 should specify and verify the actor's
energy/update mathematics. M04 should train conventional baselines against these
same semantics before interpreting a Hopfield controller's performance.

The Gymnasium interface follows its [environment creation documentation](https://gymnasium.farama.org/tutorials/gymnasium_basics/environment_creation/)
and its distinction between [termination and time-limit truncation](https://gymnasium.farama.org/tutorials/gymnasium_basics/handling_time_limits/).
